AI Use Guide

You'll work with an AI coding assistant a lot in this course, and in a few places not at all. This page shows which is which, and how to say what you used.

About 5 minutesApplies to every module
🤖

The short version

  1. AI can help you build and think. In most of this course, directing an assistant well is part of the skill.
  2. You answer for what you submit. If it's in your work, you should be able to explain it and say whether it's right.
  3. Say what you used. Disclosing AI use doesn't cost you points.

The reason is the job itself. A twin acts on physical systems, and the people who build them spend their time judging whether a model, a simulation or an agent can be trusted. You can't judge work you couldn't do or explain yourself. That's why every AIC starts with an assistant and ends by hand.

Keep your keys out of it

Never paste an API key or your .env file into an AI chat or prompt, and don't let the assistant copy your key into any file other than .env.

What's allowed in each activity

Activity and AI ruleWhat that means
AIC · Create / Scaffold
Expected		Build with an AI coding assistant (GitHub Copilot in Agent mode is built into Codespaces). Paste your prompt into NOTES.md, with anything you changed in what it built.
AIC · Evaluate
Your judgment		The assistant can suggest tests, but you run them and report what actually happened, including what it got wrong or missed.
AIC · Analyze
Your own words		You can ask an AI to explain code while you learn it. The walkthrough you submit is your own explanation, not a paraphrase of AI output.
AIC · Understand & Refine
No AI		Done by hand. Every lesson labels this part "No AI assistant for this part." Turn the assistant off for it.
AIC · Part B challenge
Allowed, disclose		Say in DECISIONS.md what your assistant wrote, got wrong or missed. The decisions and the reasons for them are yours to defend.
AIC · Post Mortem
No AI		Three short answers in NOTES.md after the challenge: what's still unclear, what you'd do differently, what you'll carry forward. Your own reflection, so no assistant.
Concept Debate (A and B, weekly)
AI to think, not to write		Use an AI to test your thinking. Posts are your own words and your own reasoning. Your evidence can be a run you did, a reading or a real-world example. If AI changed your thinking, say how in one line.
Knowledge Check (theory and skill, weekly)
No AI		Each quiz opens with a no-AI pledge. Retakes and hints are there so you can learn from your misses.
Term Project interview
No AI		A live, one-on-one conversation in Week 9 or 10. You explain your own twin and answer questions about it.
Term Project · System
Allowed, disclose		List every model, framework and coding assistant in your README's AI Tool Disclosure.
Term Project · Reports and presentation
Allowed, disclose		The analysis must be yours, and so must the words. Check every source and claim an AI gives you, because you answer for them.

How to say what you used

One or two plain sentences are enough: which tool, what you asked for, and what you did with the answer.

Not useful"I used AI for this assignment."
Useful"Copilot wrote the first version of twin/floor.py. It used the paho-mqtt 1.x callbacks, so on_connect never fired; I switched it to CallbackAPIVersion.VERSION2 and added a test that the twin receives a message."

How your work is checked

  • No AI detectors. They're unreliable, so your grade won't be based on one.
  • A short follow-up instead. Your instructor may ask you to explain a line of your code, make a small change to it, or say more about a post. If it's your work, that takes a minute or two.
  • If that doesn't resolve it, your instructor follows CityU's Academic Integrity Policy (University Catalog → Student Rights and Responsibilities).

Not sure whether something is allowed? Ask before you submit.
