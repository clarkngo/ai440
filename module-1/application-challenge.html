Application Challenge 1: A cold-storage warehouse's data connection

Build the data connection for a warehouse's six freezers: recorded sensor streams flow into a virtual model of each freezer that flags late, missing and impossible readings. Decide when the model should stop trusting a sensor, and which requested feature would turn the warehouse's shadow into a twin.

Ground rules

Everything here runs in GitHub Codespaces on CPU, on free tools, with no credit card and no API key. You may use an AI assistant for any part; say what it did in DECISIONS.md. See the AI Use Guide.

Starter code: Planned The AC 1 starter repository and its GitHub Classroom invite are being built and tested; the invite link goes here when they are published.

❄️

The scenario

Northgate Cold Storage (fictional) keeps frozen food in six walk-in freezers, F1 to F6, each held at −20 °C. Every freezer reports its air temperature every 30 seconds and a door event whenever its door opens or closes. Last winter a sensor in F4 froze at −20.1 °C for nine hours while the freezer warmed to −6 °C, and nobody noticed until a delivery was refused. The owner wants a virtual model of each freezer that knows what it doesn't know.

The starter has a recorded night (data/night.jsonl, 8 hours, every message with its sensor timestamp and sequence number), a replay tool, and an empty coldstore/connection.py. Two things to know:

  • No broker is needed for the tests. The replay feeds messages straight to your code, in the order and at the times they arrived. --mqtt sends them through Mosquitto in your Codespace instead, for the live run.
  • The recorded night contains faults on purpose: a late burst, a gap in one freezer's sequence numbers, an impossible jump and one sensor that stops changing.
📐

The spec

Every requirement below is checked by a provided test.

PartMust doMust not do
FreezerModel.on_message(topic, payload)Accepts coldstore/<freezer>/temp and coldstore/<freezer>/door messages with v, ts and seq, and keeps each freezer's latest temperature, door state and arrival time.Trust arrival order, or drop a message silently.
FreezerModel.state(freezer, now)Returns temp, door, age_s and a list of flags: late (more than 90 s from sensor to arrival), missing (a gap in seq) and impossible (below −40 °C, above +10 °C, or changing faster than 2 °C a minute with the door shut).Report a value with no age, or clear a flag before a good reading arrives.
Unknown stateReturns status="unknown" for a freezer whose data can no longer be trusted, by your rule (below).Report status="ok" for F4 during the recorded stuck period.

The open decision: when does a freezer become unknown?

The owner's only instruction: "I'd rather get a false alarm at 3 a.m. than lose a freezer of fish." A sensor that hasn't changed can be a stuck sensor or a very steady freezer. How long, and under what conditions (door shut? compressor running? other freezers changing?), before you stop trusting it? The provided tests only check that F4 is unknown by the end of its stuck period. You decide the rule, implement it, prove it with your own tests, and defend it in DECISIONS.md.

🗂️

The owner's three requests

The owner also asked for three features. You don't build these. In your memo, decide for each whether it makes the system a digital shadow, a digital twin or neither, and what it would let the system control on its own.

  1. A dashboard of all six freezers that emails the manager when one passes −15 °C.
  2. Switching a freezer to its backup compressor automatically when the model predicts it can't hold −18 °C.
  3. A weekly report of door-open minutes per freezer.
🪜

Steps & what to submit

1Open it and run the tests

Open your own AC 1 repo in GitHub Codespaces, then run:

pytest

Almost everything fails with NotImplementedError. That's your to-do list.

2Build the connection

Fill in coldstore/connection.py until the provided tests pass. You may use an AI assistant; tell it to work in this existing workspace and not to change the tests, and read what it writes: you'll defend it.

3Make the unknown-state call

Implement your rule. Write at least two tests in tests/test_my_rules.py: one showing your rule on F4, and one showing a steady but healthy freezer is not marked unknown too early.

4Replay the night

Run the whole night through your model and save the timeline:

python -m coldstore.replay data/night.jsonl --speed 600 > replay.txt

Copy the part around F4 into REPLAY.md and mark the moment it goes unknown. Optional: repeat with docker compose up -d mosquitto and --mqtt.

5Write the memo

Fill in DECISIONS.md, about one page: your unknown-state rule and why, your call on each of the three requests, and what your AI assistant wrote, got wrong or missed.

Submit (in Brightspace)

  • coldstore/connection.py and tests/test_my_rules.py
  • The output of pytest, all tests passing (including yours)
  • REPLAY.md: one replay of the recorded night, with the moment a freezer goes unknown
  • DECISIONS.md: your unknown-state rule and why, and your shadow-or-twin call on each of the three features, with what each would let the system control

Each criterion is graded on one part of your submission, at one of three levels.

How this differs from HOS 1's Understand & Refine: there you extended code you already understood, by hand, with no AI. Here the business, the code and the decisions are new, AI help is allowed, and half the grade (Analysis and Evaluation) is for the calls you make, not the typing.

🧯

Common snags

  • missing fires for every message — compare each seq with the previous one for that freezer and topic, not across all freezers.
  • late never fires during the replay — compare the sensor's ts with the replay's arrival time (now), not with time.time(). The replay runs faster than real time.
  • A door-open warm-up is flagged impossible — the 2 °C-a-minute limit applies only with the door shut. Track the door state per freezer.
  • The assistant changed a test to make it pass — undo it with git checkout tests/. The provided tests are the spec.
