The project at a glance

Group of threeAll 4 AICs

The use case. A small packaging line, a conveyor, a pick-and-place arm and a heat sealer, loses a few percent of its packs to bad seals, mostly after film changes and jams, when the sealer's jaw runs too cold or too hot. Two thermocouples sit at the jaw's edges; nobody can see its core. Packaging Cell Twin monitors the line live, predicts the jaw's core temperature, and may slow the conveyor on its own, only inside limits its what-if tests proved. It's the course description's manufacturing-cell example, cut to a size a team can finish in a Codespace.

DecisionMade byWhy
Slow the conveyor when the jaw is predicted to run coldTwin inside its envelopeFrequent, small and reversible, and the sweep tested the whole speed range.
Stop the line when the light curtain is brokenDeterministic codeA fixed safety rule. No model belongs anywhere near it.
Change the heater setpointA personNot covered by the what-if tests yet, so not inside the envelope.
Restart after a stop-and-alertA personSomeone has to find out whether the sensor or the model was wrong.
AICWhat it is in Packaging Cell TwinLitmus test: why it's really that AIC
AIC 1 · pipeline & twinRecorded sensor traces replayed over MQTT into the twin's state, InfluxDB and a Grafana dashboard, with stuck, jump and stale checks.The twin knows each reading's age, and one action travels back to the cell over MQTT.
AIC 2 · physics-informed modelA PINN of heat through the jaw, with its diffusivity learned from a week of readings.The heat equation is in the loss, and it beats a data-only model between the two sensors.
AIC 3 · simulation & synthetic dataThe arm's cell in MuJoCo, exported to OpenUSD; a pick detector trained on 1,800 BlenderProc images.Both were tested on held-out worlds they weren't built from, and the team reports the gap.
AIC 4 · what-if & safe decisionsA SimPy sweep of jams, heater faults and line speeds; an ADK agent that may only set conveyor speed, through an envelope callback; a JSONL action log.Every limit points to a sweep result, including a combined failure, and the stop-and-alert rule is shown working.

    Three releases: what was in each

    Phase 1 · 2 · 3

    Packaging Cell Twin didn't appear in Week 10. Each phase released a working system version and a report, the same as yours will.

    End of Week 3 · Phase 1

    End of Week 7 · Phase 2

    System v0.7

    • The twin's state with quality checks, and the jaw PINN reading live data

    End of Week 10 · Phase 3

    System v1.0

    • The what-if sweep, the envelope from it, and the agent acting inside it
    • Stop-and-alert shown working with an injected sensor fault
    • Held-out results for the PINN, the arm policy and the detector
    • A README execution guide with an architecture diagram and an AI tool disclosure
    • Final report, presentation and demo video
    What they cut, and said so

    Running the arm's policy on the twin's live data (it runs only in simulation), and putting the heater setpoint under the agent (no sweep covers it yet). Both are on the "what didn't make it" slide. That's the Integration Debate in practice: they kept the autonomy narrow and the evidence complete.

    What makes this a strong example

    Use it as a checklist
    • The autonomy is narrow and justified. One action, in a tested range, with a named person for everything else.
    • Every AIC is real, not a token mention. For each one the team can point to a specific part of the twin and say why it's that AIC (the litmus test in the second table).
    • One loop shows all four. The demo follows a reading from a sensor to an action and back, instead of touring features one by one.
    • It shows a failure on purpose. The frozen sensor proves the stop-and-alert rule earned its place, which is more convincing than a demo where nothing goes wrong.
    • Its numbers include the cost. The envelope blocked three fine actions, and the presenters say so.
    • It's honest about the physical entity. The cell is simulated and its traces replayed, and the report says what that can't show.
    • Every member is visible. Owners per area, commits from all three, everyone speaks, and the title card names who built what.

    Now plan yours: map your twin, write your backlog, then use the freeze checklist and the Demo plan.
