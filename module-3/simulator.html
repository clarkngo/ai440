What this teaches

  • Success at home isn't success. A fixed-world policy learns the simulator; held-out worlds show the gap.
  • Randomization trades a little for a lot: a few points at home for robustness everywhere else.
  • Too wide is worse than none. If no single behavior works across the range, nothing is learned.
  • Realism is a budget. Varied images usually beat beautiful ones, and cost far less CPU time.

This is a simplified browser model for building intuition, not the real build: no physics engine or policy runs; the success rates come from a simple model of how training ranges cover test worlds, and render times are estimates for two CPU cores.
