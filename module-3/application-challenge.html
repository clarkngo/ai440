Application Challenge 3: A recycling line that misreads dirty items

A recycling line's classifier was trained on clean synthetic images and fails on dirty, crushed items under flickering light. Generate a better synthetic training set within a fixed render budget, decide what to randomize and how widely, and explain what synthetic images still can't teach it.

Ground rules

Everything here runs in GitHub Codespaces on CPU, on free tools, with no credit card and no API key. You may use an AI assistant for any part; say what it did in DECISIONS.md. See the AI Use Guide.

Starter code: Planned The AC 3 starter repository and its GitHub Classroom invite are being built and tested; the invite link goes here when they are published.

♻️

The scenario

Cascade Recovery (fictional) sorts PET bottles, aluminum cans and cardboard on a conveyor, with a camera over the belt and air jets that push each item into its bin. A vendor trained the classifier on 3,000 clean rendered images. On the belt it misreads crushed cans as cardboard and sends a quarter of the bottles to the wrong bin, especially when the old fluorescent lights flicker.

The starter has a fast CPU renderer (recycle/render.py, NumPy and Pillow, about 200 small images a second), a small classifier and its training script, and a test set of 300 belt-like images made with settings you can't see. Two things to know:

  • The render budget is fixed at 3,000 images, the number the vendor used. More images isn't an option; better ones are.
  • The renderer exposes ten properties you can set or randomize: lighting level, flicker, dirt, crushing, labels, belt texture, background, camera jitter, motion blur and occlusion.
📐

The spec

Every requirement below is checked by a provided test.

PartMust doMust not do
generate(n=3000, seed=0) in recycle/generate.pyReturns n labeled images from recycle.render, balanced across the three classes, with the settings used for each image saved to data/settings.csv.Exceed 3,000 images, or use the test set in any way.
The trained classifierTrained by python -m recycle.train on your images, reaches at least 85% accuracy on the hidden test set, and at least 75% on each class.Change the classifier's architecture or training script.
ReproducibilityThe same seed gives the same images and the same accuracy.Depend on unseeded randomness.

The open decision: what to randomize, and how widely?

The plant manager's only instruction: "A can in the cardboard bale costs us the whole bale." Every property you randomize widely takes images away from the others, and too wide a range teaches nothing. The provided tests only check the accuracy thresholds and the budget. You choose the settings, prove what they buy with your own tests, and defend them in DECISIONS.md.

🧪

What synthetic images can't teach

You don't build these. In your memo, decide for each case whether better synthetic data could cover it, and if not, what would (real images, a different sensor, or a person).

  1. A crushed can inside a clear plastic bag.
  2. A new bottle shape the plant starts receiving next month.
  3. The camera's lens slowly fogging up over a humid shift.
  4. Items piled two deep on the belt.
🪜

Steps & what to submit

1Open it and run the tests

Open your own AC 3 repo in GitHub Codespaces, then run:

pytest

The accuracy tests fail with the vendor's clean images, and generate() raises NotImplementedError.

2Generate a better set

Fill in recycle/generate.py, train with python -m recycle.train (about a minute on CPU) and iterate until the provided tests pass. You may use an AI assistant; tell it to work in this existing workspace and not to change the tests or the classifier, and read what it writes.

3Make the randomization call

Settle your settings. Write at least two tests in tests/test_my_rules.py: one showing a choice of yours matters (accuracy with and without it), and one for the edge you think is most likely to go wrong.

4Run it at two settings

Train with your settings and with one deliberately too-wide setting, and record both:

python -m recycle.compare --settings mine.json --settings too_wide.json

Add the accuracy per class for each to RUNS.md.

5Write the memo

Fill in DECISIONS.md, about one page: what you randomized and why, what the budget made you give up, your call on each of the four cases, and what your AI assistant wrote, got wrong or missed.

Submit (in Brightspace)

  • recycle/generate.py and tests/test_my_rules.py
  • The output of pytest, all tests passing (including yours)
  • RUNS.md: your randomization settings and one too-wide setting, each with its test accuracy
  • DECISIONS.md: what you randomized and why, what the 3,000-image budget made you give up, and what synthetic images can't teach this classifier

How this differs from HOS 3's Understand & Refine: there you extended code you already understood, by hand, with no AI. Here the business, the code and the decisions are new, AI help is allowed, and half the grade (Analysis and Evaluation) is for the calls you make, not the typing.

🧯

Common snags

  • Accuracy jumps between runs — something isn't seeded. Pass one numpy.random.Generator through generate() into every render call.
  • One class collapses below 75% — check the class balance in data/settings.csv, and whether one property (crushing, for cans) is randomized so widely that cans stop looking like cans.
  • Rendering takes minutes, not seconds — motion blur and occlusion are the slow properties. Render at the starter's 64 × 64; don't raise the resolution.
  • The assistant changed a test to make it pass — undo it with git checkout tests/. The provided tests are the spec.
