Build a digital twin that can be trusted, one module at a time

Ten weeks, five modules. In Modules 1–4 you build the parts of a digital twin: a real-time pipeline that keeps it in sync with its sensors, a physics-informed model of how the system behaves, a simulation that trains and tests physical AI, and what-if tests with a safety envelope for autonomous decisions. In Module 5 you combine all four into one twin for a use case you choose, on your own or with a group.

How each module runs: two weeks

Each module centres on an AIC (AI Challenge), worth 10% of your grade, in two parts: Part A: Build in Week 1 and Part B: Apply in Week 2, which ends with an individual challenge in a new scenario. Both parts go in one GitHub Classroom repo, and your last push before the end of Week 2 is your submission (there is no Brightspace upload for the AIC). It's graded once: 60 points for the build, 40 for the challenge. Every week also has a Concept Debate and a Knowledge Check, graded separately.

The AIC used to be called the AI Challenge (AIC). Some page addresses still say AIC, such as hos-guide.html.
