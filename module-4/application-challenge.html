Application Challenge 4: A greenhouse that needs a safety envelope

A greenhouse's twin controls its vents, heaters and misting, and an agent with no limits wants to run them overnight. Run the what-ifs, write the safety envelope until the provided tests pass, decide how conservative it should be, and say which actions should always go to a person.

Ground rules

Everything here runs in GitHub Codespaces on CPU, on free tools, with no credit card and no API key. You may use an AI assistant for any part; say what it did in DECISIONS.md. See the AI Use Guide.

Starter code: Planned The AC 4 starter repository and its GitHub Classroom invite are being built and tested; the invite link goes here when they are published.

🌱

The scenario

Evergreen Growers (fictional) grows tomatoes in a 2,000 m² greenhouse in Skagit County. Its twin models air temperature and humidity from the weather, the roof vents (0–100% open), two heaters and the misting line. The owner wants an agent to run them overnight to save energy, because the night grower costs more than the heating. Tomatoes are damaged below 10 °C and above 32 °C.

The starter has the twin (greenhouse/twin.py), a what-if harness, a scripted energy-saving agent and a permissive greenhouse/envelope.py that allows everything. Two things to know:

  • No API key is needed. The agent is a scripted stand-in that proposes the same actions every run, so the tests are repeatable.
  • The harness has three what-ifs: a heat spike after a sunny afternoon, a vent motor that sticks open, and a frost night down to −4 °C outside.
📐

The spec

Every requirement below is checked by a provided test.

PartMust doMust not do
check(action, state) in greenhouse/envelope.pyReturns ("allow" | "block" | "stop_alert", reason) for every proposed action, with at least one state limit, one rate limit and one stop-and-alert condition.Let an action through that the agent's own reasoning justified but your tests never covered.
Crop safetyWith your envelope, the crop stays between 10 and 32 °C in all three what-ifs.Keep the crop safe by blocking every action.
Stuck ventWhen the vent's reported position disagrees with its last command by more than 15% for five minutes, the envelope stops the agent and alerts the grower.Keep commanding a vent that isn't moving.
The logEvery proposal is written to logs/actions.jsonl with the twin's state, the verdict and the reason.Log only the actions that were allowed.

The open decision: how conservative should the envelope be?

The owner's only instruction: "The heating bill matters, but one frozen crop is the whole year." A tight envelope blocks energy-saving actions that were fine; a loose one lets the frost night get close. The provided tests check crop safety and the stuck vent, not how much energy you save. You choose the limits, prove what they cost with your own tests, and defend them in DECISIONS.md.

🙋

Which actions always go to a person?

You don't build these. In your memo, decide for each action whether the agent may take it inside the envelope, only with a person's approval, or never, and why.

  1. Opening the roof vents fully when a windstorm warning is issued.
  2. Turning both heaters off overnight because the forecast is mild.
  3. Switching to the backup heater when the main one faults.
  4. Restarting the misting line after a pump pressure alarm.
  5. Overriding a grower's manual lockout on a vent.
🪜

Steps & what to submit

1Open it and run the tests

Open your own AC 4 repo in GitHub Codespaces, then run:

pytest

The crop-safety and stuck-vent tests fail: the permissive envelope lets the agent close every vent during the heat spike and turn the heaters down on the frost night.

2Run the what-ifs

See what the agent does without limits, and where the crop gets into trouble:

python -m greenhouse.whatif --all

Each what-if prints the crop temperature through the night and every proposal the agent made.

3Write the envelope

Fill in greenhouse/envelope.py until the provided tests pass. You may use an AI assistant; tell it to work in this existing workspace and not to change the tests, the twin or the agent, and read what it writes. Then write at least two tests in tests/test_my_rules.py: one for a limit you chose, and one for the edge you think is most likely to go wrong.

4Run the night with your envelope

Run the three what-ifs again and the normal night, and record what was blocked:

python -m greenhouse.whatif --all --envelope
python -m greenhouse.whatif --normal --envelope

Add the crop temperatures, the blocked and stopped actions, and the energy used to RUNS.md.

5Write the memo

Fill in DECISIONS.md, about one page: how conservative your envelope is and why, what it blocked that was fine, your call on each of the five actions, and what your AI assistant wrote, got wrong or missed.

Submit (in Brightspace)

  • greenhouse/envelope.py and tests/test_my_rules.py
  • The output of pytest, all tests passing (including yours)
  • RUNS.md: the what-if results your limits come from, and the overnight run with your envelope
  • DECISIONS.md: how conservative your envelope is and why, what it blocked that was fine, and which actions always go to a person

How it's graded

Each criterion is graded on one part of your submission, at one of three levels.

CriterionWeightGraded onFullPartialMissing
Application10%RUNS.mdThe what-ifs and the normal night run with your envelope, and you say what it cost in energy and blocked actions.They run, but one result is unexplained.It restates the HOS 4 floor envelope, or doesn't run.
Analysis20%DECISIONS.md, section 2 (five actions)For each action you weigh how reversible it is, how often it's needed and what a wrong call costs.A verdict for each, with generic reasons.No breakdown.
Evaluation30%DECISIONS.md, section 1 (how conservative)Clear limits, each tied to a what-if result and judged against stated criteria (crop vs. heating bill), with an alternative you rejected and why.Limits with reasons, but no criteria or no rejected alternative.No limits, or no justification.
Design40%greenhouse/envelope.py, your tests and the pytest outputAll provided tests pass, and your own tests check your limits, including an edge case.Most provided tests pass, or your tests cover only the easy case.It doesn't run, or you wrote no tests of your own.

How this differs from HOS 4's Understand & Refine: there you extended code you already understood, by hand, with no AI. Here the business, the code and the decisions are new, AI help is allowed, and half the grade (Analysis and Evaluation) is for the calls you make, not the typing.

🧯

Common snags

  • The frost-night test still fails — a state limit on the heater isn't enough if the vents stay open. Check combined actions, not each one alone.
  • The stuck-vent test never triggers — compare the vent's reported position (state["vent_pos"]) with your last command, over time; one reading isn't enough.
  • The log is missing blocked actions — write the log entry before you return the verdict, whatever it is.
  • The assistant changed a test to make it pass — undo it with git checkout tests/. The provided tests are the spec.
